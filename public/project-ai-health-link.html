AI Health Link Canada

A responsive app that helps people figure out what to do when they feel unwell: assess symptoms, keep an eye on vitals, and find the right level of care in the Canadian system — without pretending to be a doctor.

Sole designer, end to end
3 months

The problem

“Is this an emergency, a clinic visit, or nothing?”

Getting medical help in Canada usually starts with uncertainty. Walk-in clinics have long waits, ERs are overloaded, and telehealth lines vary by province. When something feels wrong, most people’s first move is a search engine — which either tells them they’re fine or that they’re dying.

I wanted to design something that sits in that gap: a tool that helps you understand your symptoms, tracks the vitals you can measure at home, and points you to the right door — pharmacy, clinic, telehealth, or ER.

The hardest constraint wasn’t visual. It was that a triage app that’s wrong in the reassuring direction can hurt someone. Every design decision below was shaped by that.

Design decisions

Structuring the app around urgency, not features

The information architecture went through several versions. The obvious structure — tabs for Symptoms, Vitals, Doctors — organized the app the way the system thinks, not the way a worried person thinks. A worried person has one question: how serious is this?
