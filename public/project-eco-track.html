ECO Track

A three-week sprint on a question I kept circling back to: can an app show people the environmental cost of their spending without making them feel so guilty they delete it by Thursday?

The problem

Carbon data has a motivation problem

Most carbon-tracking apps fail the same way: they tell you everything you do is bad. Your groceries are bad, your commute is bad, your one flight a year is catastrophic. People don’t change behaviour under a constant stream of bad news — they stop looking.

Spending data is a genuinely good proxy for footprint, and it’s already being collected. The design question wasn’t can we compute a number — it was can we frame that number so people come back to it.

The second problem was trust. To work, the app needs your transaction history — which means asking for the most sensitive data a person has, on first launch, before you’ve earned anything.

Design decisions

Two bets: a rating, not a lecture — and privacy up front

The ECO-Rating

Instead of showing raw kilograms of CO² (meaningless to most people), I designed a simple per-purchase rating. The goal was glanceability: you should understand your week in two seconds, the way a credit score works without you understanding the formula.
